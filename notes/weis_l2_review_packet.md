Prepared 2026-07-28 for obligation `N-L2-AUDIT-001`. This is the reading order
and the checklist for an independent review; the derivation itself is
[`weis_l2_full_height_one.md`](weis_l2_full_height_one.md) and is not repeated

**What is being asked of you.** Not "does the program run". The programs run and
agree with each other, which is exactly the situation in which this repository
has twice been wrong before (`RETRACTIONS.md`). What is being asked is whether
the *mathematics* composes: whether the finite facts the programs decide are the

Everything below is checkable with pencil and paper except where marked

## 1. The claim, fully quantified

Over Σ = {a, b}, let

    L2 = L( ( a b* a  |  b a* b (a b* a)* b a* b )* ).

**Claim.** The generalized star-height of L2 is exactly 1: there is a
generalized regular expression for L2 with star-nesting depth 1, and there is
none with depth 0.

This is a statement about **one language**. It is *not* `HeightOneForGroup
(C₂ × S₄)`, and the packet must not be read as one; the syntactic group's
full-alphabet problem is untouched and open.

## 2. Provenance — why this language is worth a referee's time

PST 1992 §8 "Further results" (p. 29 of the preprint,
`irif.fr/~jep/PDF/StarHeight.pdf`) reads verbatim:

> Unfortunately, it is even not known whether there exist languages of
> star-height greater than or equal to 2! A possible candidate is
> L = (ab\*a ∪ ba\*b(ab\*a)\*ba\*b)\*.
> Notice that if A is the automaton represented on Figure 6, then
> L = {u ∈ A\* | 0·u = 0 and |p(u)|₍₀,b₎ ≡ 0 mod 2}.

So the language is the candidate proposed by the authors of the main
group-theoretic results on the problem, and left open by them. Weis 2011 §4.4
p.115 reprints it ("proposed in [30]") and also leaves full L2 open, proving
height one only for the restricted subset with ≥ 4 a's between b's
(`WEIS-L2-OPEN-01`).

**Ground truth is anchored to the primary source, not to a transcription.**
Until 2026-07-28 every evaluation path in this repository started from the same
printed string, so a transcription error would have been invisible to all of
them. PST's own second description now closes that: Figure 6 is a drawing and
does not survive PDF text extraction, but its transition table is forced by the
two constraints (states {0,1,2}; the characterization must denote the printed
language), and the reconstruction

    a : 0 ↔ 1, 2 fixed        b : 0 ↔ 2, 1 fixed        (transition monoid S₃)

is **[machine]** proved to denote exactly the printed language by product
reachability, with no word bound —
`python3 scripts/research/weis_l2_pst_crosscheck.py` (`WEIS-L2-PST-SRC-01`).
Five controls fire. *Falsifier:* a separating word between the two descriptions.

Worth noticing on paper, because it explains why PST could not finish it: their
own description is an **arrow-counting language over an S₃ automaton**, which is
precisely the shape their Theorem 7.6 handles when the base is elementary
abelian 2. S₃ is not, and that is the gap.

## 3. The construction, in pieces you can check

### 3.1 L2 is a group language, and which one

**[machine]** The printed expression compiles to a 6-state minimal DFA, which is
the walk automaton of

    ψ₆(a) = (0 1)(3 4),   ψ₆(b) = (0 2 3 5),   start = accept = 0,

so L2 = φ⁻¹(Stab(0)) for φ : Σ* ↠ G = ⟨ψ₆(a), ψ₆(b)⟩ ≤ S₆, |G| = 48,
[G : Stab(0)] = 6. *Falsifier:* a DFA with a different state count, or a
disagreement between the compiled automaton and `re` on the printed string.

Paper check: 6 states and index 6 must agree, and they do.

### 3.2 The group data — all of it verifiable by hand

Re-derived independently of the repository's script on 2026-07-28; every number
below was reproduced:

| fact | value |
|---|---|
| \|G\| | 48 |
| Z(G) | order 2 |
| central fixed-point-free involutions | **exactly one**, z = (0 3)(1 4)(2 5) |
| antipodal pairs | {0,3}, {1,4}, {2,5} |
| G′ | order 12 (≅ A₄) |
| z ∈ G′ ? | **no** |
| faces (3-subsets with no antipodal pair) | 8 |
| z-orbits on faces = diagonals | 4 |
| ψ(a) on diagonals | (D₂ D₃), a transposition |
| ψ(b) on diagonals | (D₀ D₃ D₂ D₁), a 4-cycle |

G ≅ C₂ × S₄ with the C₂ generated by z; ψ : G → S₄ is the projection.

### 3.3 The anchor pair, and why the stars collapse

ψ(a) is supported on {D₂, D₃}; ψ(b) is a single 4-cycle. So the diagonals moved
by *both* letters are exactly D₂ and D₃ — the anchors. **This is the whole
idea**: on the original 6 points no such pair exists (vertices 0 and 3 are moved
by both letters but the off-anchor part still carries b-cycles), and passing to
the quotient action on the 4 diagonals is what creates one.

Fix an anchor d and delete it from the 4-point walk graph. **Trace this by hand;
it takes a minute and it is the load-bearing step.**

- Delete D₂. Remaining D₀, D₁, D₃. a: D₀→D₀, D₁→D₁, D₃→(deleted).
  b: D₁→D₀, D₀→D₃, D₃→(deleted). The only cycles are the two a-self-loops.
- Delete D₃. Remaining D₀, D₁, D₂. a: D₀→D₀, D₁→D₁, D₂→(deleted).
  b: D₂→D₁, D₁→D₀, D₀→(deleted). Same.

Since `a* = ¬(⊤ b ⊤)` is star-free, every escape language and every first-return
language is star-free. Explicitly:

    R_{D₂} = (a | b a* b a* b)(a | b)
    R_{D₃} = (a | b) a  |  (a | b) b a* b a* b

*Checked by hand during preparation and correct.* From D₂ the exits are a→D₃ and
b→D₁; from D₃ **both** letters return to D₂, which is why the `a` branch must
return immediately and why the `b` branch runs D₂→D₁→(a*)→D₀→(a*)→D₃→D₂.

Then K_d = (R_d)* has star-height 1, and W_d(x) = K_d·S_d(x) is the set of words
whose d-walk ends at x, by unique factorization at the last visit to d.

### 3.4 Separating the 48 elements — and the count is tight

ψ alone does not separate G: Stab_ψ(D₂) ∩ Stab_ψ(D₃) = {1, (D₀ D₁)}. Add the two
letter parities (|w|_a mod 2, |w|_b mod 2).

**A one-line proof, which the machine check does not give you.** ⟨transposition,
4-cycle⟩ with the transposition inside the cycle generates S₄, so ψ is onto and
|ker ψ| = 48/24 = 2, i.e. ker ψ = ⟨z⟩. The parity map is well defined on G
because it factors through G^ab and z ∉ G′; and **parity(z) = (1, 1) ≠ (0,0)**
**[machine, reproduced independently]**. Hence ker ψ ∩ ker(parity) = 1 and
(parity, ψ) is injective. ∎

**The tightness check, which is the cheapest way to catch an error here.**
The signature is (parity ∈ 4 values, ψ(w)(D₂) ∈ 4, ψ(w)(D₃) ∈ 3 remaining):

    4 × 4 × 3 = 48 = |G|

The injection is a **bijection with zero slack**. Any miscount of the group, the
diagonals, the anchors or the parities breaks this equality. It holds.

### 3.5 Assembly and the lower bound

Each fiber φ⁻¹(g) is an intersection of four height-≤1 languages; L2 is the
union of the 8 fibers over Stab(0). Boolean operations do not raise generalized
star-height, so gsh(L2) ≤ 1.

Lower bound: the syntactic monoid is a nontrivial group, hence not aperiodic,
hence L2 is not star-free (Schützenberger 1965). So gsh(L2) = 1 exactly.

## 6. Where a referee should push hardest

1. **The Weis 2009 talk.** This is the sharpest prior-art risk and it is not
   closed. The abstract of "The Power of One Star" (UMass Theory Seminar,
   2009-11-03) says verbatim that the speaker will "present a series of four
   candidate languages, and show that all of these languages have generalized
   star height one". The 2011 thesis leaves full L2 and L3 **open**, so the
   two cannot both be about the same four languages; the repository's reading is
   that the talk's candidates were restricted variants (`WEIS-TALK-01`,
   `notes/weis_2011_primary_audit.md` §2.4). **That reading is an inference.**
   The slides were not retrieved (the author's UMass page is 404) and no paper
   exists. *Recommended action before any external write-up: write to Weis or to
   Barrington and ask.* If the talk did settle full L2, this result is not new —
   though it would remain unpublished, and the construction here would still be
   the only recorded one.
2. **§3.3, the unique-factorization step.** W_d(x) = K_d·S_d(x) needs the
   decomposition at the *last* visit to d to be unique. It is, but it is the
   place where an anchor argument usually goes wrong, and the machine check
   verifies the assembled language rather than this lemma in isolation.
3. **Star-freeness of R_d as a language, not as an expression.** The printed
   forms contain `a*`. The claim is that the language is star-free; check the
   aperiodicity of the transition monoid yourself rather than reading the
   expression.
4. **The 8-fiber union.** |Stab(0)| = 8 and the union is over Stab(0), not over
   a coset. Cheap to get backwards.
5. **rsh(L2) = 2 rests on two cited theorems.** Only `UNIV-SUBGRP-01` is proved
   here. If you review one thing in §6 of the derivation note, review whether
   Thm. 7.10 of Lombardy–Sakarovitch is being applied within its hypotheses
   (regular *group* language).
